How a strategy
gets tested.

Four steps, in this order, every time. The order is the method — running them in a different sequence would make the results unfalsifiable.

01

Write it down

The rule, every parameter that will be tried, the data, the costs, and the score it has to beat. All of it, before any data is loaded.

02

Seal it

The document is hashed and the hash is timestamped onto the Bitcoin blockchain. Anyone can verify later that it existed, unaltered, before the test ran.

03

Run everything

Every combination is tested, not just the ones that look promising. Dead variants that never trade are excluded from the count so they do not inflate the bar.

04

Score against luck

The best result is compared with what the best of that many random strategies would have scored anyway. Only a result that clears that, by the margin fixed in step one, counts.

The number almost nobody reports

Every record states the smallest real edge the test could have detected at all. Without it, "we found nothing" and "our instrument was too blunt to see anything" look identical — and most published negative results do not distinguish them.

Sealed, not merely claimed

Two of the records here are marked void: one was an arithmetic mirror that could not have been an independent test, and three were scored from a run that had crashed. They stay published with the reason attached, because a registry that quietly removes its own errors is asking not to be believed.

What a failure does and does not mean

A strategy that does not pass has not been proven worthless. It has been shown not to produce an edge large enough for this test to distinguish from chance, on this data, with these costs. Smaller edges may exist and would be invisible here. That limit is printed on every record rather than buried.
